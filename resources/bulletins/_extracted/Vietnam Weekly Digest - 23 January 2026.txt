Vietnam Emerges as Diplomatic Anchor for Southeast Asia: Scholar
Vietnam has moved beyond its role as a manufacturing hub to become a key diplomatic anchor for Southeast Asia, said Ms. Julia Roknifard, a senior lecturer in international relations at Taylor’s University. In an interview with Vietnam News Agency, Mr. Roknifard pointed out that since joining ASEAN in 1995, Vietnam has become an essential link in global supply chains through export-led growth and sustained social stability, enabling the country to pursue a more confident foreign policy. She added that Vietnam’s commitment to multilateralism, non-interference and neutrality in regional conflicts has strengthened its credibility and made it a constructive force in building ASEAN consensus. She believed that Vietnam is expected to play a larger role in shaping regional security cooperation, including maritime security, and could act as an “anchor” for stability while helping advance ASEAN connectivity and integration in the years ahead. On January 20, border forces from Vietnam and Laos carried out a joint patrol along a section of their shared border in Vietnam’s Nghe An province, local authorities said. Vietnam, EU Launch Legal, Justice-related Project with Five-Year Roadmap
On January 19-20, Vietnam’s Ministry of Justice and the European Union launched the EU JULE II project during a conference in northern province of Phu Tho, setting a five-year roadmap to strengthen Vietnam’s legal and judicial systems, state media reported. The EU JULE II project supports Vietnam’s priorities in governance and human rights by improving lawmaking quality, judicial services and institutional capacity, building on results from the first phase and aligning with national Party resolutions on the rule-of-law state. It targets citizens and vulnerable groups, including women, children, ethnic minorities and low-income communities, while directly supporting agencies such as the Ministry of Justice, Supreme People’s Court, Supreme People’s Procuracy and Ministry of Public Security. EU JULE II consists of two components, including technical assistance for state institutions and funding support for qualified non-state legal organizations to expand access to justice, legal aid, and rights protection under Vietnam’s legal framework. Authorities said that expected outcomes include a more transparent, consistent and independent legal system aligned with international human rights conventions, alongside stronger professional capacity and public awareness across Vietnam’s justice sector. For the 2026-2030 period, the project will prioritize technical support for lawmaking, legal workforce training and digital transformation in legal and judicial operations, with 2026 identified as a key year for translating strategy into action. Conference participants also discussed annual planning procedures, budget standards under EU and UN rules, expert selection mechanisms and coordination roles among project units, UNDP and UNICEF to ensure effective implementation and oversight. Diplomat Urges Strategic Autonomy as Vietnam Navigates Shifting World Order
Ahead of the 14th National Party Congress, former Deputy Foreign Minister Pham Quang Vinh, Vietnamese ambassador to the U.S. in 2014-2018, said Vietnam must strengthen strategic autonomy and broaden partnerships as the world moves toward a multipolar order marked by intense competition and uncertainty. In an interview with Ho Chi Minh City Law (Phap Luat TP.HCM), Ambassador Vinh said today’s multipolarity is no longer defined only by rivalry among major powers but also by the rise of new centers such as Europe, Japan, South Korea and India. That shift, he warned, increases pressure on developing countries to choose sides, making independence and strategic self-reliance essential. Vietnam has diplomatic relations with 194 countries and economic ties with more than 200 economies, including 34 comprehensive and strategic partners. This broad network provides favorable political and economic conditions, he said, but the challenge is how to turn those relationships into concrete gains. He stressed that opportunities now are intertwined with risks, requiring Vietnam to build strong domestic capacity to withstand external shocks. Vietnam should pursue foreign relations based on overlapping interests, identify shared priorities with partners and expand networks of interlinked cooperation. As Vietnam’s development goals rise, Mr. Vinh said the country must prioritize high-quality external resources aligned with innovation, digital transformation and green growth, rather than indiscriminate inflows. He also called for more proactive international engagement as global governance and multilateralism face growing strain. Draft documents for the 14th Party Congress, he noted, reaffirm diplomacy as a pioneering, comprehensive, and modern pillar, alongside national defense and security, with economic diplomacy playing a central role in advancing national development. Vietnam Sees Major Opportunities as High Seas Treaty Takes Effect
The entry into force of the United Nations (UN) High Seas Treaty will open unprecedented opportunities for cooperation in marine biodiversity conservation and the sustainable development of the blue economy, said Deputy Foreign Minister Nguyen Minh Vu, In an interview released by the Vietnam News Agency, Mr. Vu, also chair of the 35th Meeting of States Parties to the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), noted that Vietnam signed the treaty on the first day it opened for signature and completed ratification procedures in June 2025. The agreement formally takes effect on January 17, 2026. Becoming an early party demonstrates Vietnam’s strong commitment to protecting oceans and sustainably using marine biodiversity, Mr. Vu said, adding that the treaty will help Vietnam expand international cooperation, access advanced data and technologies, and strengthen marine scientific research and environmental management. As a treaty member, Vietnam plans to actively engage in all four pillars of implementation: fair access to and benefit-sharing from marine genetic resources, establishment of marine protected areas, environmental impact assessments for activities beyond national jurisdiction, and capacity building and technology transfer. Vietnam will also represent the interests of developing countries and climate-vulnerable states in line with UNCLOS. Mr. Vu said that Vietnam will continue refining its legal framework, strengthening interagency coordination, and mobilizing scientists, businesses and the public to ensure effective implementation, supporting both national and global goals for a sustainable ocean economy. Vietnam’s Party Congress as Milestone toward Rising Middle-Power Role: Expert
Vietnam’s 14th National Party Congress will be a critical milestone in strengthening the country’s strategic autonomy and shaping its emergence as a rising middle power in the region, Australian scholar Carl Thayer said. In an interview with the local Znews newspaper, Prof. Thayer from the Australian Defense Force Academy said that the Party congress comes at a pivotal moment of global instability and marks an opportunity for an orderly leadership transition, continued political streamlining, and renewed economic reform, aimed at boosting productivity and long-term growth. Vietnam Designates Lead Agencies to Implement EVFTA, CPTPP, UKVFTA
Vietnam’s Deputy Prime Minister Bui Thanh Son has signed decisions designating focal agencies to coordinate and implement three key free trade agreements: the EU–Vietnam Free Trade Agreement (EVFTA), the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), and the UK–Vietnam Free Trade Agreement (UKVFTA). Under the decisions, the Ministry of Industry and Trade (MoIT) is appointed as the central coordinating body for all three agreements. The ministry will oversee implementation across ministries and agencies, consolidate progress reports for submission to the Prime Minister, and coordinate proposals and the receipt of technical assistance from foreign partners. For EVFTA, MoIT will lead on trade remedies, non-tariff barriers, competition policy, state-owned and privileged enterprises, and institutional and final provisions. The Ministry of Finance will be responsible for customs, trade facilitation and government procurement, while the Ministry of Science and Technology will oversee technical barriers to trade and industrial property. The Ministry of Agriculture and Environment will handle market access for agricultural goods and sanitary and phytosanitary measures. For CPTPP, MoIT will act as the focal point for cross-border trade in services, competition policy, trade remedies, dispute settlement, and cooperation and capacity building. The Ministry of Finance will oversee tariffs, customs, trade facilitation, and information related to state-owned enterprises, while the Ministry of Home Affairs will lead on labor-related commitments. Under UKVFTA, MoIT will coordinate implementation of provisions on trade remedies, non-tariff barriers, competition policy, services, e-commerce and dispute settlement. The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism will serve as the focal agency for copyright and related rights, while the Ministry of Justice will lead on transparency-related obligations. (

U.S. Sets 1.08% Provisional Countervailing Duty on Vietnamese Rebar
The U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC) has imposed a provisional countervailing duty of 1.08% on concrete reinforcing steel bar (rebar) imports from Vietnam, the lowest rate among the countries under investigation, according to a preliminary ruling released by the agency. The countervailing duty probe, launched on June 30, 2025 at the request of the U.S. domestic steel industry, covers rebar imports from Vietnam, Algeria, and Egypt. DOC selected Hoa Phat Group as Vietnam’s sole mandatory respondent, along with 10 affiliated companies participating in the investigation. The preliminary determination applies the same 1.08% duty rate to Hoa Phat, its affiliates and other Vietnamese exporters. By comparison, DOC set much higher provisional subsidy rates for the other countries, imposing duties of 72.94% on Algerian producers and 29.51% on Egyptian exporters. The significantly lower rate for Vietnam is seen as giving Vietnamese steelmakers a clear competitive edge in the U.S. market. Alongside the preliminary finding, DOC initiated investigations into nine additional alleged subsidy programs related to rebar products. The agency is expected to issue supplemental questionnaires to Vietnamese enterprises and the Vietnamese government to gather further information ahead of its final determination. Vietnam’s Trade Remedies Authority under the Ministry of Industry and Trade urged relevant companies to continue full cooperation with DOC, prepare comprehensive documentation, respond promptly to follow-up questionnaires, participate in on-site verifications and submit comments on the preliminary conclusions where appropriate. The U.S. decision followed a separate ruling by Australia’s Anti-Dumping Commission (ADC) on December 16, 2025, which concluded an anti-dumping investigation into rebar imports from Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, Turkey, and Thailand. After verification, the ADC found Hoa Phat’s dumping margin to be below 2%, insufficient to justify anti-dumping measures, and terminated the case without imposing duties. The consecutive outcomes in the U.S. and Australia underscore the competitiveness of Vietnam’s steel industry and are expected to support Hoa Phat’s efforts to maintain and expand exports in major international markets. Vietnam’s central Nghe An province will select an investor under a special mechanism for Quynh Lap LNG project while Khanh Hoa province is still evaluating bidding documents for the Ca Na LNG project, local media reported. Nghe An’s Department of Finance has published evaluation criteria for investor proposals for the Quynh Lap LNG power project. The project includes a 1,500 MW power plant, an LNG storage facility with a tank capacity of about 250,000 cubic meters, a regasification system, a dedicated LNG port, and synchronized technical infrastructure. The plant’s LNG demand is estimated at about 1.15 million tons per year. Total investment is about VND55.95 trillion ($2.19 billion), including VND55.64 trillion for project execution and VND305 billion for site clearance and resettlement, if applicable. Under a decision approved by Nghe An People’s Committee on January 15, 2026, the project will select an investor under a special case. Additional investor documents will be received on January 30, 2026. The selected investor must prioritize a central LNG hub model to supply gas-fired power plants in the region and share infrastructure such as LNG ports, transport, and power distribution facilities. The investor must also meet timelines under the adjusted Power Development Plan VIII and directives from the Prime Minister, ensuring project approval and preparation procedures are completed within 12 months. The project is scheduled for operation during 2025-2030. In Khanh Hoa province, the Department of Industry and Trade opened bids for the 1,500 MW Ca Na LNG Power Plant in late July 2025. The sole bidder was the Trung Nam - Sideros River consortium, offering an electricity price of VND3,294.22/kWh. Provincial authorities said the Department of Finance is still evaluating the bid. According to the schedule, Ca Na LNG is expected to begin construction in late 2026 and enter operation in the first quarter of 2030. (

Vietnam Nghe An Sets Criteria to Select Investor for $2.2B MW LNG Project
Vietnam’s central Nghe An province has issued investor selection criteria for the 1,500 MW Quynh Lap LNG power project, setting a formal basis to advance a key power project planned for operation during 2025-2030, local media reported. The People’s Committee approved a scoring framework to rank project proposals, requiring investors to meet at least 60% of total points across core and detailed criteria before consideration. The project covers 152.88 hectares and includes two 750 MW combined-cycle gas turbine units, LNG storage, regasification systems, a dedicated port, and supporting infrastructure, using about 1.15 million tons of LNG annually. Total investment is estimated at VND55.95 trillion ($2.2 billion), with investors required to contribute at least 15% in equity, while the operating term is set at 50 years from land allocation or lease approval. Evaluation criteria focus on construction and operation capacity, schedule compliance, and experience in gas-fired IPP projects and LNG supply chains to ensure timely commissioning. Provincial authorities expect the Quynh Lap LNG project to strengthen energy security and attract investment to the North Central region as Vietnam expands gas-fired power capacity. (
