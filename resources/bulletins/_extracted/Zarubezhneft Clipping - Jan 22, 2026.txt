Vietnam Caps Central Budget Projects at 3,000 by 2030
Vietnam plans to cap central government budget projects at 3,000 by 2030 as part of reforms to improve capital efficiency and attract private investment, Finance Minister Nguyen Van Thang said at the 14th National Party Congress in Hanoi on January 21.The policy builds on 2021–2025 results, when state budget revenue reached nearly VND9,900 trillion ($388.24 billion) and public investment totaled about VND3,400 trillion, while the number of central-funded projects fell sharply from around 11,000 to 4,600.The next phase targets average GDP growth of at least 10% a year through 2030, with total social investment at about 40% of GDP and public investment accounting for 20%–22%, alongside tighter fiscal discipline.The finance sector plans to issue all guidance for newly passed laws by the first quarter (Q1) of 2026, deepen financial markets, expand access to international investment funds, and promote instruments such as green bonds and carbon markets.Officials said restructuring public investment and limiting project numbers will help redirect state capital toward large-scale, strategic projects while mobilizing greater participation from private and foreign investors. Vietnam Fuel Distributors Ready for E10 Rollout
Vietnam’s fuel distributors are preparing to roll out E10 gasoline nationwide from June 2026, as the Ministry of Industry and Trade and major suppliers finalize infrastructure, supply chains, and quality controls to replace E5 RON92, local media reported.Vietnam National Petroleum Group (Petrolimex) said it operates eight biofuel blending depots nationwide and plans E10 and E5 output of about 455,000 cubic meters per month.Military Petroleum Corporation (MIPEC) is upgrading ethanol tanks and pipelines at four major depots, targeting completion by January 2026.PetroVietnam Oil Corporation (PV Oil) has completed blending and storage upgrades at 13 depots and is selling E10 at about 50 retail stations in major cities. The company said it has not received complaints on quality or vehicle performance during the pilot phase.Fuel distributors have asked regulators to shorten conformity certification timelines and adjust fuel standards to reflect higher oxygen content from ethanol blending. Petrolimex also called for early-stage price support to encourage consumer adoption as Vietnam moves toward cleaner transport fuels.Vietnam estimates annual demand of about 1.5 million cubic meters of ethanol to blend with roughly 15 million cubic meters of gasoline, based on 2024 consumption. Deputy Prime Minister Bui Thanh Son has directed ministries to review ethanol supply capacity, pricing impacts, and blending readiness. (Dau Tu)

Vietnam Plans to Let Fuel Firms Set Prices under New 2026 Decree
Vietnam plans to let fuel firms set wholesale and retail prices under a new decree in 2026, shifting price-setting power to enterprises while regulators focus on oversight, a move expected to reshape competition nationwide, local media reported.The draft decree allows wholesalers and distributors to decide prices based on market rules, while retail outlets within systems such as Vietnam National Petroleum Group (Petrolimex) and PetroVietnam Oil Corporation (PV Oil) must not exceed published prices, and independent retailers gain pricing autonomy.Vietnam Petroleum Association Chairman Bui Ngoc Bao said past pricing based on seven-day global references caused heavy losses when prices fell, leading to low discounts and supply tension despite adequate availability.Under the new mechanism, firms will declare actual costs, including inventory periods of up to 20 days, enabling realistic pricing and reducing the risk of forced losses for wholesalers.Mr. Bao said differences in procurement costs will create price variation, pushing companies to compete for retailers and consumers, while profitable operations will support stable discounts across the supply chain.Regulators will retain oversight through cost audits and inspections, ensuring transparency while allowing market forces to manage supply, pricing, and competition in the fuel sector. (Bao Phap Luat)

Vietnam Energy Agencies Hold Jan 21 Forum to Implement Resolution 70
Vietnam energy regulators and industry groups held a forum in Hanoi on January 21 to advance implementation of Politburo Resolution 70, focusing on power supply security, investment, and policy reform to support long-term economic growth, local media reported.The Electricity Authority of Vietnam said Resolution 70 sets energy as a strategic pillar through 2030 with a 2045 outlook, requiring supply to stay ahead of demand while ensuring independence, efficiency, and alignment with the global energy transition.Officials noted the power system has expanded capacity, diversified fuel sources, and boosted renewable energy, but rising electricity demand and grid constraints continue to pressure supply-demand balance during peak periods.The Ministry of Industry and Trade has issued action plans and proposed new National Assembly resolutions to remove policy bottlenecks, accelerate Power Development Plan VIII projects, and strengthen the legal framework for energy investment.Forum discussions focused on capital mobilization, investor selection for large-scale power projects, renewable energy and offshore wind development, and grid and storage investment to support Vietnam’s net-zero emissions target by 2050. (Vietnam Plus, Nhan Dan, Tin Tuc)

14th Party Congress Repositions Oil, Gas in Vietnam Energy Security Strategy
Vietnam’s oil and gas sector expects the 14th National Party Congress to redefine its strategic role in national energy security and the energy transition, as outlined by Vietnam Oil and Gas Association Chairman Nguyen Quoc Thap in a recent interview.Mr. Thap said policies under the 13th Party Congress helped Vietnam maintain energy stability despite global price shocks, supported by Power Development Plan VIII, demand management, and gradual electricity price adjustments.However, he noted persistent bottlenecks, including slow grid investment, limited flexible power capacity, and policy barriers that delay final investment decisions and create risks of localized power shortages.Mr. Thap said National Assembly Resolution 253/2025/QH15 marked a shift toward institution-building, aligning with Politburo Resolution 70 to secure long-term energy supply and economic resilience.Looking ahead, he called for repositioning oil and gas as a pillar of energy security, deeper LNG and gas development, stronger technology transfer, and clearer recognition of the sector’s role in Vietnam’s Net Zero pathway. Vietnam Electricity Targets May 2026 Commercial Operation for 1,400-MW Plant
State utility Vietnam Electricity (EVN), the country’s sole power distributor and the biggest producer, is moving its VND41.13 trillion ($1.61 billion) Quang Trach I coal power plant toward grid connection in Quang Tri province in January, with commercial operation targeted for May, strengthening power supply for central Vietnam.The 1,400-MW project, comprising two coal-fired units, has reached about 98% completion after construction began in December 2022, with thousands of workers operating around the clock to finalize testing and commissioning.Once fully operational, the plant is expected to generate around 9.1 billion kWh annually, using technology with efficiency above 43% to reduce fuel use and emissions.Vietnam Electricity is also advancing EPC selection for the 1,500-MW Quang Trach II LNG project planned for the first quarter (Q1) of 2026, while Quang Trach III LNG has been approved for construction starting in Q4 2026. Experts Discuss Environmental Impacts of Vietnam Nuclear Power Projects
Experts gathered at a seminar in Vietnam’s central Khanh Hoa province on January 21 to discuss measures to avoid environmental impacts from nuclear power projects set to be developed in the locality, local media reported.Mr. Nguyen Thanh Binh, deputy head of the Ninh Thuan 2 Nuclear Power Plant Preparation Board at PetroVietnam, said environmental protection, safety, and livelihoods are core concerns. He said the seminar aimed to provide transparent information on environmental impacts, international safety standards, and Vietnam’s legal framework to build public trust.Mr. Pham Tuan Hiep, expert at EDF and Secretary General of the Vietnam Overseas Nuclear Experts Network (VietNuc), said nuclear plants must strictly control environmental safety, especially marine ecosystems. He cited France’s Gravelines Nuclear Power Plant, with 5,500 MW capacity, as an example of effective cooling water and radiation control under international standards.Experts also highlighted radiation monitoring, public disclosure of radiation data, and the “defense-in-depth” principle. Nuclear waste volumes were cited as significantly lower than coal ash waste over comparable periods.U.S.-based former power engineer Tran Si Chuong stressed that safety culture, human factors, and social acceptance are more critical than technology alone. He said strong governance, workforce training, and public consensus are essential.Vietnam’s legal framework under the 2020 Environmental Protection Law and the 2025 Atomic Energy Law requires the strictest environmental impact assessments, including radiation risk scenarios, real-time radiation monitoring, and full life-cycle environmental responsibility. (PetroTimes, Tuoi Tre, SGGP, Bao Khanh Hoa)

Vietnam Pushes Hydrogen Energy Strategy Toward 2030, 2050 Targets
Vietnam is accelerating implementation of its hydrogen energy strategy, the Ministry of Industry and Trade said in a January 19 report, as the government seeks to build a long-term low-carbon energy framework.Domestic hydrogen output remains at about 500,000 tons per year, mainly gray and brown hydrogen from refining and fertilizer plants, while multiple green hydrogen projects in southern provinces remain in the research and preparation phase.Hydrogen demand is concentrated in fertilizer and refinery operations, with only limited use in steel, glass, and electronics, as green hydrogen applications in power and transport have yet to enter commercial deployment.The ministry said enterprises are studying fuel switching, hydrogen-ammonia blending, carbon capture, and use of existing gas infrastructure, alongside stronger international cooperation to mobilize capital and technology.The government plans policy incentives, planning integration, green finance access, and updated technical standards, aiming to attract investment and align hydrogen development with national energy and climate goals. Vietnam Vinh Long Seeks UK Investment for Renewable Energy Projects
The authorities of Vietnam’s southern Vinh Long province met with UK trade officials in on January 21 to promote investment cooperation, focusing on renewable energy and industrial development as part of the province’s growth strategy.Provincial leaders said the newly consolidated province spans more than 6,200 square kilometers with a 130-kilometer coastline, offering strong potential for wind power, marine infrastructure, and clean energy investment.Vinh Long is prioritizing investment in industrial parks, deep agricultural processing, supporting industries, and renewable energy, while committing to streamlined procedures and incentives for British investors.UK representatives said British companies are actively pursuing global wind power projects and are prepared to support renewable energy development in Vinh Long through investment, technology transfer, and workforce training, aligning with Vietnam’s emissions reduction and net-zero targets. Vietnam Advances Waste-to-Energy Projects across Localities
Vietnam has advanced waste-to-energy projects across major cities by January 2026, as local governments and private investors expanded treatment capacity to cut landfilling and recover energy from rising urban waste, local media reported.Southern Ho Chi Minh City is developing the Tam Sinh Nghia and VietStar plants at the Northwest Cu Chi complex, each designed to process about 2,000 tons per day, with operations planned from 2026 and 2027 to reduce landfill dependence.Central Danang City is seeking investors for a PPP waste-to-energy plant handling about 1,000 tons per day and generating around 20 MW, backed by investment of more than VND2.7 trillion ($105.88 million), with operations targeted for late 2026.Northern Haiphong City plans to bring its Dinh Vu waste-to-energy plant online in 2026, with initial capacity of about 1,000 tons per day and investment near VND2.5 trillion, followed by expansion through 2030.Central Binh Dinh province approved the Long My project with capacity of about 500 tons per day and output of 15 MW, while Ca Mau plans a 600-ton-per-day plant in Nam Can to ease pressure on existing landfills.National policy reforms now mandate waste sorting at source and target landfill rates below 30%, while cities such as Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi are revising waste service pricing to support recycling and energy recovery. (pcgroup.vn)
