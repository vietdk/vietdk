Record renewable energy financing in the Caribbean: Bladex lends USD 425m to six AES Dominicana p
rojects
Bladex, a multinational bank founded in 1979 by the central banks of Latin America and the Caribbean, has announced its role as the lead lender in a USD 425 million financing package granted to AES Dominicana. The transaction is aimed at refinancin
g and strengthening a portfolio of six renewable energy projects in the Dominican Republic: five solar photovoltaic (PV) plants and one wind power facility.
All six assets are already 100% operational, delivering clean electricity to the national grid and reinforcing the resilience and capacity of the Dominican power system to meet rising energy demand.
The financing involved six leading banks from Panama and
the Dominican Republic, with Bladex providing the largest individual contribution. This position consolidates Bladex’s role as a key financier of sustainable infrastructure across the region.
The transaction builds on the recent entry of TotalEnergies, w
hich acquired 50% of the solar, wind and battery energy storage system (BESS) portfolio of AES Dominicana Renewables Energy. Through this deal, the French multinational not only expanded its strategic partnership with AES in the Caribbean, but also strengt
hened its renewable energy footprint in the Dominican Republic.
AES’s renewable portfolio in the country includes more than 1 GW of contracted wind, solar and BESS projects, of which 410 MW are already operational or under construction, supplying electric
ity under long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs). The pipeline also comprises over 500 MW of solar and wind capacity under development, alongside BESS projects designed to mitigate intermittency and enhance grid stability.
Part of the new financing wi
ll be allocated to the installation of battery energy storage systems at four of the plants, further improving grid reliability, operational efficiency and flexibility. According to the companies involved, this transaction represents the largest renewable
energy loan ever granted in the Caribbean.
“This operation marks a milestone for the region and reaffirms Bladex’s commitment to sustainability, the energy transition and the strengthening of critical infrastructure. Projects like this drive long-term eco
nomic, environmental and social value for our countries,” said Samuel Canineu, Executive Vice President of Business at Bladex.
Edwin De los Santos, CEO of ADRE and President of AES in the Dominican Republic, highlighted that the agreement “reinforces AES
Dominicana’s leadership in renewable energy”.
“At AES, we are firmly committed to advancing the energy transition while ensuring the development of reliable, secure and flexible power systems. The confidence shown by Bladex and regional banks is a clear s
ign of the robustness of our portfolio and the positive impact these plants are already delivering for the country,” he added.
Bladex had previously participated in the original construction financing of these renewable assets, supporting AES Dominicana f
rom the early development stages of this strategic portfolio. In 2023, the bank acted as structurer and lender in two transactions totalling USD 160 million, aimed at refinancing and strengthening solar and wind projects.
These included a USD 40 million e
quity loan to AES España and an additional USD 120 million financing, split equally between Bladex and Banco Popular. Those operations laid the foundations for the growth and consolidation of AES Dominicana’s clean generation platform.

Vietnam Advances Waste-to-Energy Projects across Localities
Vietnam has advanced waste-to-energy projects across major cities by January 2026, as local governments and private investors expanded treatment capacity to cut landfilling and recover energy from rising urban waste, local media reported.
Southern Ho Chi
Minh City is developing the Tam Sinh Nghia and VietStar plants at the Northwest Cu Chi complex, each designed to process about 2,000 tons per day, with operations planned from 2026 and 2027 to reduce landfill dependence.
Central Danang City is seeking inv
estors for a PPP waste-to-energy plant handling about 1,000 tons per day and generating around 20 MW, backed by investment of more than VND2.7 trillion ($105.88 million), with operations targeted for late 2026.
Northern Haiphong City plans to bring its Di
nh Vu waste-to-energy plant online in 2026, with initial capacity of about 1,000 tons per day and investment near VND2.5 trillion, followed by expansion through 2030.
Central Binh Dinh province approved the Long My project with capacity of about 500 tons
per day and output of 15 MW, while Ca Mau plans a 600-ton-per-day plant in Nam Can to ease pressure on existing landfills.
National policy reforms now mandate waste sorting at source and target landfill rates below 30%, while cities such as Ho Chi Minh Ci
ty and Hanoi are revising waste service pricing to support recycling and energy recovery.

Experts Discuss Environmental Impacts of Vietnam Nuclear Power Projects
Experts gathered at a seminar in Vietnam’s central Khanh Hoa province on January 21 to discuss measures to avoid environmental impacts from nuclear power projects set to be developed in the locality, local media reported.
Mr. Nguyen Thanh Binh, deputy hea
d of the Ninh Thuan 2 Nuclear Power Plant Preparation Board at PetroVietnam, said environmental protection, safety, and livelihoods are core concerns. He said the seminar aimed to provide transparent information on environmental impacts, international safe
ty standards, and Vietnam’s legal framework to build public trust.
Mr. Pham Tuan Hiep, expert at EDF and Secretary General of the Vietnam Overseas Nuclear Experts Network (VietNuc), said nuclear plants must strictly control environmental safety, especiall
y marine ecosystems. He cited France’s Gravelines Nuclear Power Plant, with 5,500 MW capacity, as an example of effective cooling water and radiation control under international standards.
Experts also highlighted radiation monitoring, public disclosure o
f radiation data, and the “defense-in-depth” principle. Nuclear waste volumes were cited as significantly lower than coal ash waste over comparable periods.
U.S.-based former power engineer Tran Si Chuong stressed that safety culture, human factors, and s
ocial acceptance are more critical than technology alone. He said strong governance, workforce training, and public consensus are essential.
Vietnam’s legal framework under the 2020 Environmental Protection Law and the 2025 Atomic Energy Law requires the
strictest environmental impact assessments, including radiation risk scenarios, real-time radiation monitoring, and full life-cycle environmental responsibility.

Vietnam IPs Integrate Clean Power, BESS to Attract Green FDI: GEAPP
Vietnam’s industrial parks in 2025 are integrating clean energy and battery storage to boost competitiveness and attract green FDI as the country
advances its net-zero strategy, said the Global Energy Alliance for People and Planet (GEAPP).
Vietnam has established 478 industrial parks, with 324 operating nationwide, forming the backbone of manufacturing and foreign investment, according to the Min
istry of Finance.
GEAPP said greening industrial parks is now a strategic investment as global supply chains demand lower emissions, higher renewable use, and carbon transparency across production networks.
Policy reforms, including the revised Power Dev
elopment Plan VIII, Decree 35/2022, and the new Planning Law effective March 2026, are aligning industrial, energy, and spatial planning.
Besides, new rules issued in December 2025 provide pricing and contract guidance for BESS, improving the commercial v
iability of storage projects in industrial parks with large and stable power demand.
GEAPP studies show rooftop solar of about 3.3 MWp paired with 1 MW BESS can cut peak grid use, improve cost stability, and strengthen energy resilience in industrial zones.
Through ENABLE and ESG-linked financing, GEAPP and partners are scaling clean ener
gy and storage models, positioning industrial parks as hubs of Vietnam’s low-carbon growth.

Vietnam Plans to Let Fuel Firms Set Prices under New 2026 Decree
Vietnam plans to let fuel
firms set wholesale and retail prices under a new decree in 2026, shifting price-setting power to enterprises while regulators focus on oversight, a move expected to reshape competition nationwide, local media reported.
The draft decree allows wholesalers
and distributors to decide prices based on market rules, while retail outlets within systems such as Vietnam National Petroleum Group (Petrolimex) and PetroVietnam Oil Corporation (PV Oil) must not exceed published prices, and independent retailers gain p
ricing autonomy.
Vietnam Petroleum Association Chairman Bui Ngoc Bao said past pricing based on seven-day global references caused heavy losses when prices fell, leading to low discounts and supply tension despite adequate availability.
Under the new mechanism, firms will declare actual costs, including inventory periods of up to 20 days, enabling realistic pricing and reducing the risk of forced losses for wholesalers.
Mr. Bao said differences in procurement costs will create price vari
ation, pushing companies to compete for retailers and consumers, while profitable operations will support stable discounts across the supply chain.
Regulators will retain oversight through cost audits and inspections, ensuring transparency while allowing
market forces to manage supply, pricing, and competition in the fuel sector.

Vietnam Pushes Hydrogen Energy Strategy Toward 2030, 2050 Targets
Vietnam is accelerating implementation of its hydrogen energy strategy, the Ministry of Industry and Trade said in a January 19 report, as the governm
ent seeks to build a long-term low-carbon energy framework.
Domestic hydrogen output remains at about 500,000 tons per year, mainly gray and brown hydrogen from refining and fertilizer plants, while multiple green hydrogen projects in southern provinces r
emain in the research and preparation phase.
Hydrogen demand is concentrated in fertilizer and refinery operations, with only limited use in steel, glass, and electronics, as green hydrogen applications in power and transport have yet to enter commercial
deployment.
The ministry said enterprises are studying fuel switching, hydrogen-ammonia blending, carbon capture, and use of existing gas infrastructure, alongside stronger international cooperation to mobilize capital and technology.
The government plan
s policy incentives, planning integration, green finance access, and updated technical standards, aiming to attract investment and align hydrogen development with national energy and climate goals.

Vietnam Energy Agencies Hold Jan 21 Forum to Implement Resolution 70
Vietnam energy regul
ators and industry groups held a forum in Hanoi on January 21 to advance implementation of Politburo Resolution 70, focusing on power supply security, investment, and policy reform to support long-term economic growth, local media reported.
The Electricity Authority of Vietnam said Resolution 70 sets energy as a strategic pillar through 2030 with a 2045 outlook, requiring supply to stay ahead of demand while ensuring independence, efficiency, and alignment with the global energy transition.
Officials noted the power system has expanded capacity, diversified fuel sources, and boosted renewable energy, but rising electricity demand and grid constraints continue to pressure supply-demand balance during peak periods.
The Ministry of Industry and
Trade has issued action plans and proposed new National Assembly resolutions to remove policy bottlenecks, accelerate Power Development Plan VIII projects, and strengthen the legal framework for energy investment.
Forum discussions focused on capital mob
ilization, investor selection for large-scale power projects, renewable energy and offshore wind development, and grid and storage investment to support Vietnam’s net-zero emissions target by 2050.
